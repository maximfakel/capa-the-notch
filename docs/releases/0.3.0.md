Capacity Notch 0.3.0, a beta for Apple Silicon on macOS 14 or later.

**Updating from 0.2.10:** quit the running copy, replace `CapacityNotch.app`
in Applications with the new one, and open it. macOS will likely ask for
**Open Anyway** in System Settings → Privacy & Security again, since this
build is not notarized. The permissions you granted are kept.

What's new:

- **Kapa.** The surface has a character now: a small cyan bell whose face
  says what the page it stands on already says. On the Capacity card that
  needs a look it is worried, waiting for a reset, sleepy over stale
  numbers or puzzled when a Provider is disconnected. It opens its mouth
  for a file held over the closed notch, eats it when you let go and
  chews. It nods along in headphones while music plays, listens beside
  the dictation orb and is pleased when the text goes in. Its eyes follow
  the pointer, a pointer left on it brings hearts, a tap squashes it. It
  never stands in the compact strip, holds still beside the Teleprompter
  and under Reduce Motion, and draws only while it moves. It is the
  application's icon and its menu bar mark too. Turn it off in Settings →
  General.
- **Capacity as gauges.** Each Quota Window is an open arc filled to what
  is left, coloured by its Capacity Pace, with the time it comes back.
- **OpenCode Go as a third Provider**, read only after you consent, with
  at most two Providers on at once. Its key is read from OpenCode's own
  file at each request and kept nowhere.
- **The Shelf in three tabs** — Files, Screenshots and Clipboard. New
  screenshots macOS saves land under Screenshots; on its own switch, off
  by default, text you copy is kept under Clipboard, never what password
  managers mark.
- **Every open page is the same height**, so the surface no longer jumps
  as pages turn, and the closed Teleprompter shows as many lines as that
  room holds — six, or five at the largest size.
- **Nothing connected** shows the three Providers' marks and one way to
  Settings.

What's fixed:

- **An idle surface costs almost nothing.** The dictation orb went on
  drawing thirty frames a second while hidden; idle, the application now
  uses about 0.2% of a core instead of nearly 10%.
- **Pages turn cleanly.** A turning page fades at the surface's sides
  instead of being cut by its outline, and a swipe whose end was lost
  settles instead of leaving pages part-turned.
- **Music** comes back after a failed test, and a track change keeps its
  page and cover; the music page stays while the Module is on, showing the
  last track.
- **Files that overflow the Shelf** scroll under a swipe instead of
  turning the page.
